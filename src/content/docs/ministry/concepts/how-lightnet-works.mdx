title: How LightNet works

LightNet helps a Ministry with **Media Distribution**, the Distribution stage of the
[Content lifecycle](/ministry/concepts/content-lifecycle). It gives the Ministry a way to
organize its **Content** in a **Media Library**, publish information about each piece of Content, and
help people find and access it on the internet.

## From Content to a LightNet Site

A **Media Library** is a Ministry's organized collection of **Content**. When the
Ministry uses LightNet to make that collection available online, it becomes a
**LightNet Site**. In other words, the Media Library is the Content the Ministry
organizes, and the LightNet Site is the online place where people find and use it.

A LightNet Site publishes each piece of Content through a **Media Item**. The Media Item
contains information about the Content, such as its title, description, language, and
type, and includes links to the Content.

For example, a Media Item can describe a video, audio recording, image, or document.
The Content may be uploaded directly to the LightNet Site or hosted elsewhere and
linked to it, such as a video on YouTube. This allows the Ministry to publish
different kinds of Content in one place.

## Languages

LightNet uses language in two different ways. The **Site Language** is the language of the
LightNet Site's menus, buttons, and other user interface elements. The **Content Language** is
the language in which a Media Item's Content is presented. These languages can be different.

For example, a LightNet Site might use English for its menus and buttons while offering a video
in Czech. English is the Site Language, and Czech is the Content Language.

See [Languages](/ministry/concepts/languages) for more details.

## Using the LightNet Site

People use the LightNet Site to browse, search for, and access Content:

- The Homepage introduces the LightNet Site and highlights selected Content to help people
  discover the Media Library.
- The Search Page helps people find Content by searching and filtering.
- Each Media Item has a Media Item Details Page where people can read, listen to, watch,
  view, open, or download the Content, depending on its **Media Type**.

## Organizing Content

A Ministry can organize its Content in several ways, including:

- A **Media Type** describes what kind of Content a Media Item represents, such as a video,
  audio recording, image, or document. A Ministry can use format-based names such as Videos
  or Audio, or Ministry-specific names such as Sermons, Books, or Podcasts.
- A **Category** groups related Media Items by topic. For example, a Ministry might use
  categories such as Bible Studies, Christian Living, or Theology. Categories help people
  browse and filter Content, but do not place it in a particular order.
- A **Media Collection** groups related Media Items into a sequence or set. For example, a
  Ministry might use a Media Collection for the parts of a sermon series.
- A Ministry can publish equivalent versions of a piece of Content in different languages. For
  example, a Ministry might translate an English book into French. Each translation becomes its
  own Media Item. LightNet groups and links these translations, so people can move between them
  on the Media Item Details Page.

## Hosting the LightNet Site

A **Site Administrator** is the software developer who sets up and looks after a LightNet Site. They
configure the site, arrange its hosting, and keep it running.

A hosting service provides online access to the LightNet Site and any Content stored with it.

A **Content Administrator** is an editor who manages the Content of a LightNet Site. The Site
Administrator configures the site and sets up permissions so Content Administrators can use the
**Administration User Interface** to create, update, and organize Media Items and Media
Collections.

## Analytics

An analytics service collects information about how people use a LightNet Site, such as which
pages they visit and which Content they access. It is an optional paid subscription add-on. A
Ministry can decide whether the benefits justify the cost.
