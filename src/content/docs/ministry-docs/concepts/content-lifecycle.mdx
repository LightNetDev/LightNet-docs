A **Ministry** uses **Content**, such as videos, audio, images, and documents,
to share the gospel and strengthen believers in their communities. 

Content moves through four lifecycle stages:

- **Design and Production**
- **Distribution** (LightNet's focus)
- **Promotion**
- **Engagement**

## Design and Production

The Ministry plans and prepares Content for its audience. It may be the **Author** of
the Content, or it may use Content created by another person or organization. In either case,
this stage can include deciding what the audience needs, creating or selecting Content,
reviewing it, and preparing the files and information needed to share it.

Tools for this stage might include a planning document, script or storyboard, camera and
audio equipment, editing software, translation tools, and a review or approval workflow.

### Quality questions

- Is the Content clear, accurate, and fit for its purpose?
- Does it address a real need for the intended audience?
- Is the Content culturally appropriate and available in the needed languages and formats?

## Distribution

<Aside type="note" title="LightNet's scope">
LightNet focuses on the Distribution stage. The other stages remain important parts of
the Ministry's work, but they are outside the scope of LightNet.
</Aside>

Using LightNet, the Ministry makes Content available to people in its local community. A
**Media Item** describes a single piece of Content and provides access to it. LightNet helps a
Ministry organize Media Items in a **Media Library**, publish them through a **LightNet
Site**, and make them available for people to find and access online.

Tools for this stage might include LightNet and its **Administration UI** for managing the
Media Library. Cloud service providers store Content, host the site on the internet, and measure
how people use it.

### Quality questions

- Can people access the Media Item reliably on the devices and connections they use?
- Can they find it through the LightNet Site's navigation, search, and categories?
- Are the title, description, **Content Language**, and other metadata accurate and useful?

## Promotion

Through Promotion, people become aware of the Ministry's Media Items, find them,
and share them with others. Promotion helps connect the right Media Item with the people
who may benefit from it.

Tools for this stage might include word of mouth, posters with QR codes, email,
newsletters, social media, messaging apps, and partnerships with churches or community
organizations.

### Quality questions

- Are the people who need this Media Item becoming aware of it?
- Which promotion channels are bringing people to the Media Item?
- Is the message clear about what the Media Item offers and how to access it?

## Engagement

Through Engagement, people interact with Media Items individually and in community,
leading to life transformation. Engagement may include reading, watching, listening,
discussing, applying, and sharing the Content.

Tools for this stage might include discussion groups, messaging or community platforms,
in-person gatherings, follow-up conversations, surveys, and feedback forms.

### Quality questions

- Are people interacting with the Media Item in the ways the Ministry intended?
- Is the Content contributing to meaningful change in people's lives?
- What are people saying or doing that could improve this or future Media Items?

## Analytics and learning

Analytics can be used to answer the quality questions in every stage. The Ministry can use
information from its strategy and activities to learn what is working, improve its efforts,
and inform future Media Items. Analytics should support Ministry goals and be interpreted
alongside direct feedback from the people the Ministry serves. The lifecycle is a cycle:
learning from Engagement can improve the Ministry's next round of Design and Production.

Analytics and feedback may show that Content should be revised, recreated, translated, or
retired. This helps the Ministry keep its Content relevant, accurate, and useful over time.
